Today's grammar

① N + 입니까? / N + 입니다 (polite question and answer: "Is it ~?" / "It is ~")

Add -입니까? to a noun to ask politely, and -입니다 to answer politely. This is the formal, polite style of the workplace. Managers, office staff and customers use it a lot. The noun can end in a vowel or a consonant: the ending is always -입니까 / -입니다.

    있다 + -습니다 (the stem ends in a consonant)
  • d7 어머니가 편찮으셔서 고향에 갔습니다. — His mother is ill, so he went back to his hometown.
    가다 + -았습니다 → 갔습니다 (past)
  • p2 오늘 포장반은 7시까지 잔업합니다. — Packing works overtime until 7 today.
    잔업하다 + -ㅂ니다 (the stem ends in a vowel: 하다 → 합니다)

Watch out! A question ends in -까? and an answer ends in -다: 입니까? / 입니다, 있습니까? / 있습니다. Say a question with rising intonation. Also note the respectful forms used for older or senior people: 편찮으셔서 (ill) and 하셨습니다 (did), with the honorific -(으)시-.

② 몇 명입니까? (asking and counting people with native numbers + 명)

몇 means "how many". Put it before a counter: 몇 명 = how many people. To answer, use native Korean numbers + 명. Before 명, the numbers 1, 2, 3, 4 and 20 change their shape.

  • d2 네, 다섯 명입니다. — Five people.

  • d11 오늘 신입사원이 세 명 있습니다. — We have 3 new employees today.
Two number systems! People and hours use native numbers: 세 명, 일곱 시. But line numbers use Sino-Korean numbers: 2반 is read 이 반. In writing you will often see digits (2반, 7시), so learn how each one is read. Korean does not add -s to plurals: 세 명 already means "three people".

Quick tips for English speakers

  • The verb comes last. English: "Which department is it?" Korean: 어느 부서입니까? (which department · is it).
  • 입니다 is like "is / are". There is no separate word for "a / an / the", so 봉제입니다 can mean "It is sewing" or "It is the sewing department".
  • Formal polite style is common at work. Use -ㅂ니다/-습니다 with managers and in reports. With friends you will hear the softer -요 style.
  • Say 네 to agree or answer "yes". In the dialogue it also works as a polite "Yes, sir/ma'am".
  • Names of days: 오늘 (today), 어제 (yesterday), 내일 (tomorrow), 모레 (the day after tomorrow).

More from the dialogue: particles and patterns

Particles (small words after nouns)

이/가 = subject (결근이, 어머니가) · 은/는 = topic (포장반은, 인원은) · 을/를 = object (인원을) · 에 = place / time (2반에, 고향에) · 까지 = until (7시까지)

After a consonant: 이 · 은 · 을 / after a vowel: 가 · 는 · 를

-(으)셔서 (respectful: because)

편찮으셔서 = 편찮다 + honorific -(으)시- + -어서 (because). 편찮다 is the respectful word for 아프다 (to be sick). Use it for someone's mother, father, or a senior.

어머니가 편찮으셔서 고향에 갔습니다. (His mother is ill, so he went home.)

수고 많이 하셨습니다

A very common work greeting at the end of the day: "Thank you for your hard work." It uses the respectful 하셨습니다. Say it to colleagues and seniors.

오늘 수고 많이 하셨습니다. (Thank you for your hard work today.)

주말 잘 보내세요

-(으)세요 is a polite request or wish. 보내세요 = please spend (time): "Have a nice weekend." It is also used for 안녕히 가세요 (goodbye).

주말 잘 보내세요. (Have a nice weekend.)

그래요? (a reaction)

Said with a rising tone to react to news: "Is that so?" / "Really?" It shows interest without asking for details.

고향이 남딘입니다. → 그래요? (His hometown is Nam Định. → Is that so?)

Reading numbers in writing

2반 = 이 반 (Sino-Korean number + 반). 7시 = 일곱 시 (native number + 시). When the lesson says 7시까지, read 일곱 시까지.

오늘 포장반은 7시까지 잔업합니다. = 오늘 포장반은 일곱 시까지 잔업합니다.
