The first consonant ㄴ

Click the letter or the examples to hear them. Press “Draw” to see the stroke order.

ㄴ
sound n
나
na
노
no

How to say it: Like the English “n”. The tip of your tongue touches the ridge behind your upper teeth.

Strokes: ① one stroke: down, then right

The second consonant ㅁ

Click the letter or the examples to hear them. Press “Draw” to see the stroke order.

ㅁ
sound m
마
ma
모
mo

How to say it: Like the English “m”. Close your lips, then open them.

Strokes: ① left side down ② across the top, then down ③ across the bottom

The third consonant ㄹ

Click the letter or the examples to hear them. Press “Draw” to see the stroke order.

ㄹ
sound r / l
라
ra
로
ro

How to say it: At the start: a quick, light tap of the tongue, between “r” and “l”, like the “tt” in American “butter”.

Strokes: ① across, then down ② across the middle ③ down, then across

The fourth consonant ㄱ

Click the letter or the examples to hear them. Press “Draw” to see the stroke order.

ㄱ
sound g / k
가
ga
고
go

How to say it: A soft “k” with no puff of air. Between vowels, it sounds almost like “g”.

Strokes: ① one stroke: across, then down

How to say it: A soft “t” with no puff of air. Between vowels, it sounds like “d”.

Strokes: ① across the top ② down, then across

How to say it: A soft “p” with no puff of air, close to “b”. Close your lips, then open them gently.

Strokes: ① left side down ② right side down ③ across the middle ④ across the bottom

Consonants ㅅ and ㅈ

Click the letter or the examples to hear them. Press “Draw” to see the stroke order.

ㅅ
sound s
사
sa

How to say it: Like the English “s”. Before “i”, it sounds like “sh”.

Strokes: ① slant to the left ② slant to the right

ㅈ
sound j
자
ja

How to say it: Like a soft “j” in “jar”, with no puff of air.

Strokes: ① across, then slant left ② slant right
